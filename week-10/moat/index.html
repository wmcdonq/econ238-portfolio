Keep rankings out of Rochester's sales pitch

For three years, Rochester should keep college rankings out of its ads, admissions pitches, and campus publicity. It should publish clear information about teaching, costs, and student outcomes instead.

This would push back against the pressure to treat a school like a place on a scoreboard. Students and staff would share a clear rule: explain what happens here without leaning on a rank.

The most visible cost would be changing websites and printed materials. Rochester would pay for that work. The larger possible cost is losing applicants or donors who use rankings as a shortcut. Students could also lose a familiar way to describe their school.

There could be gains. Staff might spend less time selling a number. Applicants might ask better questions about classes and financial aid. But silence about rankings could also look like hiding bad news.

A cheaper option is to keep rankings in small print and explain their limits. Other schools could copy either approach. The stronger promise is to keep publishing useful facts, even when those facts look bad.

The rule would bind the university's marketing, not students' private speech.
